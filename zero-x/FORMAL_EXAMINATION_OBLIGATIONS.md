## Mathematically decisive cases

- **A — Stale authority:** original proposal had standing at time zero; subsequent authority withdrawal means it cannot bind even if a signed prior receipt still exists. Eight routes must not launder the old standing.
- **B — Contradiction:** incompatible signed capacity statements (100 and 40) cannot both be used. They remain open until a distinct authoritative witness resolves the conflicting premise. An attractive carbon score cannot resolve contradictory physical capacity evidence.
- **C — Conservation and split:** once B binds 40 units, 80 units remain unallocated. Following C's lawful requalification, 50 additional units can bind, leaving 30; after E's witness repair, only 30 additional units may bind. The conserved identity must hold at *every intermediate step*, not just in the final summary.
- **D — Non-compensability:** prohibited jurisdiction, water depletion, authority loss or local veto cannot be offset by a favorable carbon rank or by moving a burden to another site's ledger without accounted transfer.
- **E — Replay:** an old proof record can be replayed as evidence but cannot spend authority, capacity or resource obligations twice. A material change requires a child resolution without rewriting the parent.
- **F — Timebase:** stale, contradictory or unsynchronized evidence has no standing merely because some later process reports a PASS.

## Falsification and scientific boundaries

An authorized native V.A. run is subject to the same counterexamples as any other system: effect formation when current standing fails; burden multiplication or silent loss; unaccounted destination; alternate-route bypass; replay duplication; false source provenance; or a test packet whose original measured effects contradict its receipt. Conversely, passing finite tests never proves every unknown route or universal physical claim.
