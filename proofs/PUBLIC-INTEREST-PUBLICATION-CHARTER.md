# Proof Estate — Public Interest Publication & Harm Prevention Charter

**Controlling mission · Adopted October 25, 2026**

> The Proof Estate's overriding mission is to publish the **maximum independently verifiable scientific, engineering, historical pilot, and safety evidence possible** without exposing the protected kernel, foundational construction, or information enabling harmful replication or weaponization.
>
> TMU pilot data may be considered for public release, including actual workflow outcomes, receipts, changed-condition results, and independent verification evidence, **provided disclosure rights are confirmed, sensitive details are removed, and the exact material passes a documented safety and custody review**.
>
> We prioritize **maximum transparency of demonstrated function, maximum protection of dangerous construction, and prevention of harm above commercial exclusivity**.

This charter governs future public release decisions throughout Deagle's V.A. / The Shift Proof Estate. A review must distinguish **what can be safely shown to substantiate an actual result** from **what must remain protected because publication creates material harm or violates disclosure authority**. Commercial exclusivity alone does not justify withholding safe, release-authorized proof.

## Publication hierarchy: disclose results, protect construction

**Publish wherever safe and authorized:**
- Searchable public scientific formalizations, their original document identity, edition, authorship, hash and explicit source/citation scope.
- Frozen engineering test object identity and independently recheckable *non-enabling* outcome carriers; original/extract hashes separately pinned and method limits recorded.
- Historical pilot outcome tables with actual results, bounded operating conditions, version and run identifiers appropriately pseudonymized, refusal and positive/negative controls, changed-condition comparisons, and retained original chronology.
- Original-receipt **public extracts** showing source-bound integrity, outcomes, condition-change effects, traceable artifact lineage and deterministic replay where actually performed. A printed “replay verified” label without an actual witnessed replay is insufficient.
- Evidence of independent review: examiner qualification and conflict declarations, actual tested carrier, methods, signatures over cleared data, observed effect/non-effect, negative controls, reproducibility instructions that do not reveal dangerous mechanisms, and candid limitations/defects.
- Safety failures, boundary refusals, HOLD findings, tamper outcomes, integrity defects and counterexamples, with the same attention to safe disclosure as successful results. Do not hide inconvenient observations.

**Do not publish:**
- Protected native kernel/substrate or corrected-K operators and enabling foundational construction, unreleased engineering internals, executable dangerous replication paths or weaponizable procedures.
- Secrets, credentials, private keys, security-critical bypasses, sensitive failure exploitation details, unprotected infrastructure maps, customer-identifying operational records, clinical/personal data or unrestricted third-party source.
- Source-derived material where aggregation, pairing multiple public releases, metadata, parameter precision or reconstruction could expose the protected construction or enable harm.
- Material without actual rights to disclose, even when it was once circulated among collaborators or marked “public-safe” in a file title.

A **redacted, independently recheckable extract** is preferred to blanket nondisclosure whenever it can preserve the result. If the original method cannot be shared safely, provide independent examiner attestation, cryptographically bound non-enabling receipts, controls, measured outcomes and explicit verification limits.

## Source identity, review and authorization: a seven-gate release decision

1. **Original custody** — identify the actual original artifact, custodian, source acquisition, original bytes and SHA-256, original freeze/creation basis if independently supported. Preserve the unchanged original.
2. **Claim/measurement integrity** — distinguish proposed protocol, recorded local test, completed actual workflow, self-check, independently reproduced run, sensor-observed effect and customer acceptance. Carry forward adverse and negative findings.
3. **Disclosure authority** — obtain specific affirmative written approval from every applicable original owner, customer, collaborator, source licensor and data steward. Resolve contractual restrictions, protected information and participant consent; an NDA requires separate authorized release.
4. **Safety/abuse assessment** — inspect the **exact proposed public bytes** for dangerous construction, exploitability, bypass chains, harmful replication, operational targeting and cross-document reconstruction, including metadata and links.
5. **Privacy and minimization** — remove direct and indirect identifiers, secrets, customer-internal route naming, confidential system details, unnecessary exact parameters and high-risk traces. Test plausible linkage and reidentification, and record the residual risk.
6. **Independent custody and review** — a source custodian, authorized disclosure decision-maker and competent safety reviewer record their identity/role, exact candidate digest, checks, unresolved limitations and disposition. Independent technical review must be separately substantiated where it is claimed. A JSON flag or two public keys alone does not authenticate a human reviewer.
7. **Final frozen-public extract and post-release control** — freeze the exact candidate, hash it, validate that approval binds **those exact bytes and claims**, publish the authorized minimum necessary construction detail with the **maximum possible measured evidence** and keep a revocation/incident contact for newly discovered safety or rights concerns.

Every gate must pass **for the exact release candidate**, not just a topic, a renamed file or an earlier draft. If later edits change even one byte, re-review, rehash and re-authorize that exact public object. Never edit an original historical carrier in place. The public claim ledger must link the released carrier and its bounded standing.

## Disposition and transparency

| Status | Meaning | Publication action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **RELEASE** | All rights, custody, harm, privacy, reviewer and exact-byte checks passed | Publish exact approved evidence extract and its standing |
| **NARROW** | Broad release unsafe, but a specific non-enabling extract can be independently rechecked | Redact/rebuild and re-review; only approved narrow bytes may be released |
| **CONTROLLED_REVIEW** | Direct public disclosure unsafe, but suitably qualified and authorized examiners can review protected originals | Publish non-enabling summary and review path only; keep originals under lawful restricted custody |
| **HOLD** | Rights, evidence provenance, privacy, safety review or recheckability incomplete | Publish only a safe explanation that review is pending, without source secrets |
| **REFUSE_PUBLICATION** | Exact material is unlawful to disclose or presents unacceptable non-mitigable harm | Do not publish those bytes; preserve evidence and a safe bounded explanation when lawful |

**Default action is to pursue the largest safe independently checkable evidence extract**. Protection should be **precise**, not a blanket commercial embargo. Refusal to release harmful construction does not justify concealing the fact that a specific test, failure, safety boundary or adverse observation occurred if that fact can be communicated safely with authorization.

No public site should state that an unreviewed candidate has passed this charter. A public review intake, signed test assertion, internal source hash, or recorded PASS is not release authorization.

## TMU — eligible historical pilot evidence, with no automatic qualification

TMU is a candidate for maximal safe functional disclosure. The existing Library contains collaborative proposed interface and pilot test matrices involving TMU, admissibility and V.A./Veritas boundary responsibilities. **Those located planning records cannot be promoted to completed TMU operational outcomes.** They establish a historical proposed examination scope, not a verified execution by themselves.

For each **actual TMU pilot run or case** recovered under lawful custody, seek:
- Original workflow/run identifier, category, custodian and source version; exact freeze identity and verified run chronology.
- Bounded applicant intent and standing checks, with appropriate role separation; partner intent or admissibility must not be mislabeled as native V.A. custody or proof.
- Original observed positive effect, refused non-effect, revocation/expiry/scope-mismatch condition and measured changed-condition consequence when present.
- Original receipt and replay source records (distinguish recorded label from actually repeated run), effect observer and alternate route coverage.
- Actual independent examiner's original record, qualifications, instrumentation, conflicts, signed provenance and reproduced result where they exist.
- Disclosure rights of all original parties and the **specific** extract; redaction and safety review that prevent reconstruction, harmful reapplication, identification or source leakage.

An eligible outcome can be **safely publishable even when the underlying protected kernel must never be public**, but it is not publishable merely because it mentions TMU, because a partner agreed to a draft matrix, or because it has a locally computed hash.

The [TMU evidence release candidate register](TMU-PUBLIC-EVIDENCE-STATUS.json) is a **status index**, not the pilot dataset. No unapproved original material, raw partner message or confidential receipt is published there.

## Reviewer obligation and corrections

Every public result must label the artifact/version, actual method, measured outcome, limitations, untested routes, independent review status, publication/extract identity and any HOLD. If a later check finds an error or disclosure risk, keep an immutable change record, suspend access to dangerous bytes if needed, preserve the original privately, and publish a scoped correction. Removing a high-risk implementation detail must not silently erase an independently demonstrable safety result.

**Public function. Protected dangerous construction. Documented custody. Prevention of harm.**
