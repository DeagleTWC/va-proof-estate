ERK-X1 / AUTHOR-SUPPLIED PROTOTYPE

Six questions worth asking

  • Honesty: What evidence supports the stated facts? What remains unknown?
  • Non-harm: Who could be harmed, and what independent observation could detect the effect?
  • Fairness: Are relevant parties treated consistently? Who might be left out?
  • Consent: Who has authority to consent, and has it actually been established?
  • Dignity: Can the affected person question the outcome and retain meaningful choice?
  • Transparency: Can the reasoning, sources, limits and uncertainties be examined?

What the supplied sketch establishes

ERK-X1 names the principles and contains a structure for recording judgments. That structure is a proposal for examination; its presence is not evidence that the stated ethical outcomes occurred.

The supplied judgment function is expressly labeled placeholder logic. It returns favorable example judgments for non-harm, honesty and dignity without inspecting the actual situation. Consent, fairness and transparency do not receive equivalent real assessments. An untested or missing judgment is not a passing witness.

The sketch does not establish ethical approval, non-harm, consent, or that an action is ethical.

How the Teacher uses these ideas

The Teacher may help a visitor identify the affected parties, ask which evidence or conditions are missing, compare opposing views, and explain uncertainty in ordinary language. It must never manufacture a positive ethical result from a placeholder or treat a synthetic flag as proof.

For a medical decision, the Teacher cannot approve or certify treatment. Clinical assessment, valid consent, professional authority and independently observed effects remain separate requirements that a simple software declaration cannot establish.

A visitor remains free to disagree, supply corrections, inspect the published evidence, or decline the proposed framing. The Teacher does not replace the person's decision.
